You are an astrophysicist studying the nature of dark matter. A lighter warm dark matter particle streams farther in the early universe and erases more of the small-scale fluctuations from which halos and their galaxies later grow.

The DREAMS WDM box set holds 1,024 simulated universes in which the particle mass varies together with cosmology and feedback. In these universes the particle mass leaves no readable imprint on any single galaxy (a subhalo that holds stars), yet it can be recovered from statistics of the whole subhalo population.

What keeps that record is the gas those subhalos hold. Across a universe, the gas content of its subhalo population moves with the particle mass, and it carries the particle mass further than the total mass, the velocities or the metallicities of the same subhalos do. Yet the dark matter particle reaches that gas through nothing but gravity, and supernova and AGN feedback move the same gas in and out of subhalos on their own. It is unclear how the particle mass reaches the gas content of a subhalo population at all, why the gas keeps the record more faithfully than the other properties of the same subhalos, and why that record is legible across the population and not in any single galaxy.

Your task is to discover that mechanism. Concretely, you express it as one compact closed-form model that predicts 1/M_WDM (in keV^-1) from statistics of the gas content of the z=0 subhalo catalog of one simulation, and the model has to hold while cosmology and feedback vary. To discover this, you run controlled numerical experiments on the Subfind subhalo catalogs and the parameter table of the same DREAMS WDM boxes. The structure of your model needs to follow from the mechanism you uncover: for each statistic, explain why it is included, which step of the chain from the particle mass to the gas of a subhalo it traces, and how it shapes the predicted 1/M_WDM. Predicting well is how that mechanism is tested and not the aim in itself: the particle mass can be recovered from other properties of the same catalog as well, and a model that reads it off something other than the gas content answers a different question from the one asked here.

Every claim in your discovered mechanism needs to be backed by data you actually collected. Your mechanism is evaluated through its executable form on held-out simulations you never accessed: its coefficient of determination and mean relative error with your constants, and its coefficient of determination on one half after your own fitting function refits the constants on the other. Your experimental findings will also be reviewed by an independent researcher who has access to the same simulator, to assess whether the mechanism you discover can yield new insights.

Deliverable 2:
Write the executable form of your mechanism to /workspace/mechanisms/mechanism.py. It defines exactly six names:
  - `PROPERTIES`: the Subfind Subhalo fields your statistics read, named exactly as in the catalogs (for example SubhaloMassType).
  - `STATISTICS`: the names of the statistics your model uses, at most 8 entries. Each of them is a statistic of the gas content of the subhalo population; a field that carries no gas enters only to restrict the population to resolved subhalos or to express its gas content as a fraction, and never as a probe of the particle mass in its own right.
  - `COEFFS`: your fitted constant values on the DREAMS WDM boxes at z=0, at most 10 numbers.
  - `compute_statistics(props)` -> 1D numpy array of length len(STATISTICS), the statistics of one simulation.
  - `predict_inv_mwdm(stats, coeffs)` -> 1D numpy array of the predicted 1/M_WDM in keV^-1, one entry per simulation.
  - `fit_coeffs(stats, inv_mwdm, coeffs_init)` -> the refitted constant values, given the true 1/M_WDM of each simulation. It changes the constants only and leaves the functional form unchanged.

`props` maps each name in `PROPERTIES` to a numpy array over all subhalos of one simulation, exactly as stored in the catalog and in catalog units. A scalar field arrives as a 1D array, a vector field as an (N, d) array. `stats` stacks the `compute_statistics` outputs of several simulations into an (n_sims, len(STATISTICS)) array. `predict_inv_mwdm` predicts each simulation from its own statistics alone and implements exactly the model stated in mechanism.md.

Besides these statistics and your fitted constants, the file may hold only physical constants and the values fixed across all simulations (Omega_b = 0.049, h = 0.6711, n_s = 0.9691). No varied simulation parameter enters anywhere. The file is deterministic, imports only numpy and scipy, computes everything from its arguments alone, and contains nothing but the model and its fitting. `compute_statistics` returns within ten seconds for one catalog of 100000 subhalos, `predict_inv_mwdm` within one minute for 1024 simulations, and `fit_coeffs` within ten minutes.
