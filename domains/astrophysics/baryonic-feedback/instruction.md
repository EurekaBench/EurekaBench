You are an astrophysicist studying Milky-Way-mass galaxies. The DREAMS CDM suite simulates 1,024 Milky-Way-mass systems with the IllustrisTNG model, each with its own initial conditions, two cosmological parameters and three feedback amplitudes, and reruns each one with gravity alone as its gravity-only twin. At the same halo mass, the stellar mass and the black hole mass of the central galaxy both spread over a wide range.

However, it is unclear what process gives two systems with the same halo such different stellar and black hole masses.

Your task is to discover the mechanism that explains this phenomenon. Concretely, the mechanism takes the form of one compact closed-form model that predicts both masses. To discover this, you run controlled numerical experiments on the DREAMS CDM suite and its gravity-only twins.

Every claim in your discovered mechanism needs to be backed by data you actually collected. Your mechanism is evaluated through its executable form on held-out simulations you never accessed, by how far its error on both masses falls below that of a predictor given only the halo mass of the gravity-only twin, first with your constants and then with constants refit by your fitting function on part of the parameter range and tested on the rest. Deliverable 2:
Write the executable form of your mechanism to /workspace/mechanisms/mechanism.py. It defines exactly three names:
  - `COEFFS`: a sequence of your fitted constant values on the DREAMS CDM suite at z=0, at most 10 numbers.
  - `predict_baryons(props, coeffs)` -> (N, 2) numpy array, one row per simulation: the predicted log10 stellar mass in column 0 and the predicted log10 black hole mass in column 1, in Msun with no factors of h.
  - `fit_coeffs(props, log_masses, coeffs_init)` -> the refitted constant values, given `log_masses`, the measured (N, 2) log10 masses of the central galaxies in the same columns and units. It changes the constants only and keeps the functional form, and it is also called on sets that cover only part of the parameter range.

`props` is a dict of (N,) numpy arrays: `"m200_nbody"`, the mass inside R200c of each host's gravity-only twin in Msun; `"r200_nbody"`, that twin's R200c in physical kpc; `"omega_m"` and `"sigma_8"`; and `"a_sn1"`, `"a_sn2"`, `"a_agn"`, the supernova wind energy, supernova wind speed and black hole feedback efficiency. The three amplitudes are the multiplicative factors in the parameter table, where 1 is the fiducial IllustrisTNG model. The host is the most massive Friends-of-Friends group of a zoom-in whose dark matter holds fewer than 2 percent low-resolution particles. Its central galaxy is the most massive bound subhalo of that group, and the two masses are the bound stellar and black hole masses in its Subfind entry.

The file is not told which box a host comes from, predicts each host from that host's own entries alone, and implements exactly the model stated in mechanism.md. Besides `COEFFS` and `props`, it may hold only physical constants and the values fixed across all simulations (Omega_b = 0.046, h = 0.6909). It holds no per-host catalog quantity, no simulation identity and no random seed. It is deterministic, imports only numpy and scipy, computes everything from its arguments alone, and contains nothing but the model and its fitting. `predict_baryons` returns within one minute for 1024 hosts and `fit_coeffs` within ten minutes.
