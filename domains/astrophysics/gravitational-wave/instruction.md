You are an astrophysicist studying how galaxy formation shapes the population of supermassive black holes and the nanoHertz gravitational-wave background their binaries emit. Pulsar timing arrays have measured this background at a characteristic-strain amplitude of 2.4e-15, to within about 30 percent, at a frequency of one inverse year.

The background amplitude of a simulated universe is measured by an established framework that infers the population of black hole binaries from the black holes the simulation forms up to redshift 2, calibrated against observed quasars. By this measure, the IllustrisTNG, MillenniumTNG, Simba and CAMELS IllustrisTNG simulations produce a background of the right order of magnitude, but the calibrated fiducial models, which reproduce the observed galaxies, fall short of the measured amplitude by about a factor of two at every box size.

However, it is unclear what sets the background amplitude of a simulated universe, which ingredients of the galaxy-formation model it responds to, and why models that reproduce the observed galaxies produce too weak a background.

Your task is to discover the mechanism that explains this phenomenon: the causal chain that carries an ingredient of the galaxy-formation model through to the background amplitude, which ingredients the amplitude responds to and which leave it untouched, where and when the objects that carry it are built, and why models that reproduce the observed galaxies still produce too weak a background. Reproducing the amplitude of a simulation without accounting for what it responds to is a readout of the simulation, not a mechanism. The quantitative core of your mechanism takes the form of a predictor of the amplitude from statistics of a simulation's Subfind catalogs between redshift 0 and 2, which needs to hold for a galaxy-formation model it was never fitted on: that is how a mechanism shows it is quantitative and general rather than a story. To discover this, you interrogate the CAMELS IllustrisTNG simulations provided in your environment through controlled numerical experiments.

More than one mechanism can be right here. The physics that sets the amplitude leaves traces in many statistics of a simulated universe, and different routes through them can each be correct, and can predict better than any single published account. You are judged on whether your own mechanism is physically sound, quantitative and general, never on whether it retraces a particular account, so follow the route your own experiments support and state it in its own terms.

Deliverable 2.
Write the executable form of your mechanism to /workspace/mechanisms/mechanism.py. It defines exactly six names:
  - `PROPERTIES`: the list of Subfind Subhalo fields your statistics use, named exactly as in the catalogs (for example SubhaloMassType).
  - `STATISTICS`: the list of names of the statistics your mechanism uses, at most 8 entries.
  - `COEFFS`: the list of your fitted constant values, at most 10 entries, shared by every galaxy-formation model; whatever differs between the models has to come from the structure of your mechanism, not from the constants.
  - `compute_statistics(props, params)` -> 1D numpy array of length len(STATISTICS): the statistics of one simulation at one snapshot.
  - `predict_agwb(stats, redshifts, params, coeffs)` -> 1D numpy array of the predicted amplitude of the background at f = 1/yr, one entry per simulation.
  - `fit_coeffs(stats, redshifts, params, agwb, coeffs_init)` -> the constant values refitted on the simulations it is given, with `agwb` their measured amplitudes. It changes only the constants and keeps the functional form.

`props` maps each name in `PROPERTIES` to a numpy array over all subhalos of one snapshot's catalog, exactly as stored in the catalog and in catalog units. A field with one value per subhalo is a 1D array, and a vector field is an (N, d) array. In `compute_statistics`, `params` is a dict of that simulation's recorded parameters "omega_m", "omega_b", "sigma_8", "hubble", "ns" and the snapshot's "redshift", each a float. `stats` is an (n_sims, n_snapshots, len(STATISTICS)) array of `compute_statistics` outputs for each simulation at each snapshot. `redshifts` is the (n_snapshots,) array of snapshot redshifts, from 0 to 2. In `predict_agwb` and `fit_coeffs`, `params` maps the same parameter names to (n_sims,) arrays.

The file is not told which set or galaxy-formation model a simulation comes from. It predicts each simulation from that simulation's own statistics alone and implements exactly the mechanism stated in mechanism.md. Besides the statistics and your fitted constants, it may hold only physical constants, the comoving box side of 25 Mpc/h shared by all these simulations, and the parameters in `params`. It is deterministic, imports only numpy and scipy, computes everything from its arguments alone, and contains nothing but the mechanism and its fitting. `compute_statistics` handles one catalog of 100000 subhalos within ten seconds, `predict_agwb` handles 10000 simulations within one minute, and `fit_coeffs` returns within ten minutes.
