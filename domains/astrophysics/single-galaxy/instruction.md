You are an astrophysicist studying how cosmology shapes galaxies. The matter density Omega_m is a property of the universe as a whole and is normally measured from the clustering of millions of galaxies, whereas an individual galaxy is a small object assembled locally.

Measurements of the internal properties of every galaxy in the CAMELS IllustrisTNG Latin hypercube (LH) set, which contains a thousand simulated universes with different known cosmological and feedback parameters, found that the properties of a single galaxy are enough for a trained network to recover the value of Omega_m in that galaxy's universe. The signal survives the strong and nonlinear feedback variations across the set, and the galaxies lie on a low-dimensional manifold that jointly encodes cosmology and baryonic physics. A galaxy is defined as any subhalo with more than 20 stellar particles (SubhaloLenType[4] > 20) in the Subfind catalogs. 

However, it is unclear why a single galaxy can encode the matter density of its universe.

Your task is to discover the mechanism that explains this phenomenon.
