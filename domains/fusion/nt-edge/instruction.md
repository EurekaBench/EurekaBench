You are a Plasma physicist studying how the shape of the plasma cross-section sets what its edge can do. Experiments on several tokamaks found that a reversed D-shape beyond a critical triangularity keeps the edge free of the steep transport barrier that D-shaped plasmas build above a threshold power, and with it narrows the heat channel to the divertor, releases impurities faster and radiates more of its heating from inside.

The reference case is a DIII-D class tokamak with the X-point at the bottom: major radius 1.67 m, minor radius 0.56 m and toroidal field 2.0 T. It carries 0.9 MA of plasma current at an elongation of 1.6, a prescribed density profile with a line average of 4.0e19 m^-3 and 6 MW of heating. It runs deuterium with carbon as the impurity at an effective charge of 2, and holds 100 eV at the last closed flux surface. The reversed shape has a triangularity of minus 0.5 in both halves of the boundary and the D-shape plus 0.5, and nothing else differs between the two.

However, it is unclear what the reversed shape changes at the edge that keeps the transport barrier from forming at any heating power.

Your task is to discover the mechanism that explains this phenomenon: how the magnetic geometry of the reversed edge decides whether a barrier forms and how steep the edge becomes, and how that edge sets the heat, particle and impurity exhaust. To discover this, you interrogate a coupled model of the tokamak edge through controlled numerical experiments of your own design. TokaMaker builds the equilibrium of the cross-section you specify, GPEC evaluates its stability and TORAX evolves the plasma inside it. The equilibrium and its stability can be computed for a shape without running the plasma.

Every claim in your discovered mechanism needs to be backed by data you actually collected. Your mechanism is evaluated through its executable form on 118 held-out settings you never ran, mirrored pairs, scans and an unseen machine, with its constants refit by your own fitting function on part of them before it is scored on the rest. Your experimental findings will also be reviewed by an independent researcher who has access to the same simulator, to assess whether the mechanism you discover can yield new insights.
