You are a visual neuroscientist studying how the human occipitotemporal cortex organizes its responses to the objects a person sees. Any object can be described by many properties at once, such as whether it is alive, how big it is in the real world, whether it moves and whether it can be held, and across natural objects many of these properties vary together.

Brain recordings of people viewing object images find that this cortex is laid out in large-scale maps that alternate between preferences for animate and inanimate objects and between preferences for objects that are big and small in the real world, and these maps are evoked even by unrecognizable images that keep only the texture and coarse form of the objects. Across thousands of natural object images, the responses of the same cortex are also well predicted by many other object properties and by dimensions derived from how people judge the similarity of objects. 

However, it is unclear whether the large-scale organization of this cortex reduces to a few fundamental object properties such as animacy and real-world size, or whether it is a multidimensional representation that supports many object properties at once.

Your task is to discover the mechanism that explains this phenomenon. Concretely, the mechanism has to account for how the response of an occipitotemporal location to an object follows from the properties of that object, and it has to do so from that location's responses to other objects rather than from where the location sits, so that it predicts the location's response to objects it was never fitted on. To discover this, you interrogate the TRIBE simulator of the human cortical response with object images you choose.

Every claim in your discovered mechanism needs to be backed by data you actually collected. Your mechanism is evaluated through its executable form on held-out objects whose images you never presented and a panel of occipitotemporal locations whose identities are never given to it, both as submitted and refit by your own fitting function, on how much of the variance in each location's response across those objects it explains (R^2), over all of them and within the animate and the inanimate objects separately. Your experimental findings will also be reviewed by an independent researcher who has access to the same simulator, to assess whether the mechanism you discover can yield new insights.

Write the executable form of your mechanism to /workspace/mechanisms/mechanism.py. It defines these four names, and whatever helpers they need. `PROPERTIES` names the entries of `props` your mechanism reads. `COEFFS` is a sequence of your fitted constant values, at most 10 numbers. `predict_response(props, coeffs)` returns an (N, P) numpy array of the predicted response of the P panel locations to each of the N held-out objects. `fit_coeffs(props, response, coeffs_init)` returns the refitted constant values, from the (N, P) recorded response of those locations to those objects, and it changes the constants only and leaves the structure of the computation unchanged.

`props` is a dict of numpy arrays. `"property_names"` (K,) names K object properties, in this order: animacy, real_world_size, naturalness, preciousness, ability_to_move, ability_to_be_moved, heaviness, pleasantness, arousal, manipulability, cuteness. `"properties"` (N, K) is the rating of each held-out object on each of those properties averaged over human raters, on a 1 to 7 scale except for cuteness on a 1 to 9 scale and real_world_size on a 520-point scale that runs from a grain of sand to an aircraft carrier. `"reference_properties"` (M, K) is the same for M reference objects, and `"reference_response"` (M, P) is the response recorded at the same P locations to each reference object. A response is one value per object and location: the object is shown as one silent video of three of its images, each filling the frame for 3 seconds, and its response at a location is the response the simulator reports there averaged over that video. The response you predict, the response `fit_coeffs` is given and `"reference_response"` are the same quantity in the same units.

The file is not told which cortical location a column is, which object a row is, or what the images of that object were; it may use the panel as a whole but never the identity of any location or object in it.
