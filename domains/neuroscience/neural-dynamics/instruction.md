You are a systems neuroscientist studying how the nervous system of C. elegans organizes neural activities into a hierarchical locomotion structure. The animal's major action sequence is a cycle of forward crawling, backward crawling, and a turn that returns it to forward crawling. Each of these coarse actions is carried out through dorsoventral undulations of the body that run on a faster timescale, so that the fine motor pattern is nested inside the coarse action.

Brain-wide recordings of this animal found that a large fraction of its neurons are engaged in coordinated, low-dimensional population dynamics that cycle through the forward-backward-turn command sequence. These dynamics persist when the animal is immobilised and cannot execute the actions, and the same low-dimensional trajectory is found in every individual, with the position along it reporting the animal's command state. In moving animals, the rhythmic gait activity of the ventral cord motor neurons rides on top of these command states, while the population as a whole reports the animal's movement far better than any single neuron does. 

However, it is unclear how a distributed representation of the coarse action somehow orchestrates the granular motor patterns nested within it, and how the coarse action state, once it is broadcast across the population, reaches an individual neuron and shapes the way that neuron expresses the fast undulatory pattern.

Your task is to discover the mechanism that explains this phenomenon. Concretely, the mechanism has to account for how the coarse action state of the population organizes the fast motor representation of an individual neuron, and it has to do so from what the circuit delivers to that neuron—quantities the simulator reports—rather than from the identity of the neuron, so that it predicts the activity of a neuron it was never fitted on. To discover this, you interrogate the c302 model of the C. elegans nervous system through controlled numerical experiments designed to identify what reaches an individual neuron from the population, how the neuron combines it with the fast pattern, and what sets the outcome of that combination for a given neuron. The structure of your predictive form needs to follow from the mechanism you uncover: for each ingredient, explain why it is included, what it traces in the circuit, and how it shapes the neuron's activity.
