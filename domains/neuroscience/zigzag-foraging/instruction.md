You are a systems neuroscientist studying how C. elegans controls its body postures and finds its way to food. Its body is driven forward by a wave of dorsoventral bends running from head to tail while the head swings from side to side, so the centre of the body traces a zigzag towards the attractant, and the only thing the animal is thought to know about the food, how steeply the concentration rises at its head, rises and falls with every swing because the head that samples it is the head that swings.

Work on this pathway has shown that holding the forward command interneuron active puts the ventral cord motor neurons into alternating dorsal and ventral activity travelling from head to tail, that in water this wave falls out of coordination once the bending of the body behind each motor neuron is no longer fed back to it, and that the same cells, with nothing changed in them, undulate slowly with a short wave on firm gel and fast with a long wave in water. It has also shown that the sensory neuron detecting the attractant responds to a step of the odor signal with a calcium rise that decays within seconds while the odor persists, responds more weakly to repeated input and does not respond to a small fall, that the interneuron it drives responds to small rises with pulses of one size whatever the absolute odor concentration, and that a small change in the odor signal suppresses turning for tens of seconds while a small fall leaves turning unchanged.

However, it is unclear how the sample the head takes, one current that rises and falls with the head's own swing and reaches no motor neuron directly, is turned by the circuit into the pattern of motor neuron activity that carries the body up the gradient while the wave keeps running down the body.
